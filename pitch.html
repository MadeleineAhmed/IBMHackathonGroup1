Estimating how far someone's Parkinson's has really progressed

From noisy clinic measurements, for patients we have never seen.

The problem

The score doctors measure is not the patient's real state

At every visit, a doctor rates how severe the motor symptoms are. That number is distorted:

  1. The medicine hides the symptoms. Measured an hour after a pill, a patient looks much better than they really are.
  2. The test without medicine is often skipped. It is unpleasant, so 4 visits out of 10 have no such score.
  3. Scoring is subjective. Two doctors won't give the same number.

Our job: recover the real score at every visit.

What we noticed

Each patient follows a smooth curve. Each visit is a noisy reading of it.

One real patient from the training data. Looking at a single visit, you can be 20 points off. Looking at all of them together, the curve becomes clear.

What we built

Three ideas, each one checked against the data

  1. Use every visit of the patient, not just one. The model sees the patient's whole history: their typical score and which way it is heading. This alone halved the error.
  2. Undo the effect of the medicine. We learned how much a pill hides symptoms depending on how long ago it was taken, and adjusted for each patient's own response to the drug.
  3. Draw a smooth curve through the result. The disease only gets worse, gradually, so each patient's predictions are smoothed into a gentle rising curve.
Results

Why you can trust it

Tested the way it will be used

  1. Always tested on patients the model had never seen, exactly like the competition. No patient was ever split between learning and testing.
  2. Our own test scores matched Kaggle's every time, for every version we submitted, so the result is not luck.
  3. One change at a time, every step recorded with its report on Skore Hub, including the ideas that did not work.
In one sentence

We stopped reading each visit on its own and started reading the patient.

That, plus correcting for the medicine, took the error from 7.1 to 3.1.
